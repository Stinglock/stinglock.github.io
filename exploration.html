Exploration

Every relic/data-running option available, from a solo highsec loop to a full nullsec filament run. Each row below links to its own brief section — nullsec via filament gets the full write-up since that's the current focus.

Highsec relic/data

Astero or any T1 explo frigate, scan and hack in CONCORD space. Lowest risk, lowest pay — the filler content between everything else.

Risk: very low — CONCORD responds to any aggression. Threats: highsec gankers in cheap alpha-strike ships targeting a fit that looks expensive on d-scan; the only real defence is not flying anything ostentatious and not sitting still too long in a system that's clearly being watched.

Lowsec relic/data

Same mechanics as highsec, better loot, real risk from gate camps and roaming frigates. A covert cloak (owned, see Fleet) is the main tool here — warp cloaked past camps rather than testing them.

Risk: moderate — no CONCORD, so anyone can shoot you outright. Threats: gate camps on the pipe in and out, dedicated explorer-hunters running combat scanning probes to find you mid-hack, and fast tackle frigates that can catch a cloaky ship the instant it decloaks to hack a can.

C1–C3 wormhole relic

The same pirate-faction relic sites as null, just less competition — nobody's usually been through ahead of you. No local chat cuts both ways: no free warning, but also no easy way for a hunter to know you're there. Lower volume than null, comparable or better per-site value.

Risk: moderate — genuinely unpredictable since there's zero intel channel. Threats: a cloaky hunter already living in the hole when you arrive, a fresh K162 opening mid-session that you never scanned, and the hole itself collapsing or going critical-mass while you're deep inside with no other way out. Also: this session's own Cheetah loss was in this exact bucket, from a Sleeper Covert Research site rather than another player.

Nullsec via gates

Don't

The lowsec–null border gates are where bubble camps live. Bubbles can't be deployed in lowsec, so the null side of that gate is prime hunting ground for exactly the ship doing relic runs. Filament or a wormhole connection both skip this entirely — there's no upside to gating in that either of the other two doesn't already cover.

Risk: high — this is the single most dangerous entry method on this page. Threats: an interdiction bubble locking you in place the instant you land, a full gang already staged and waiting (this is the chokepoint everyone entering the region funnels through, so it's the easiest camp in the game to run), and no CONCORD or local warning of any kind once you're on the null side.

Ghost sites

High value, one real trap

Genuinely good loot, and the reason a Cheetah was lost this session in J134800. The cans in Covert Research sites detonate for heavy explosive damage on a failed hack — that's an environmental trap, not an NPC, and no tank fixes it. Separately, most ghost sites carry a hidden timer that spawns a real rat wave after a while, which is fightable. Run these fit for combat, hack conservatively, and leave the moment the timer shows.

Risk: high, specifically for an unfit hacking ship. Threats: the can detonation itself (survivable in a tanked hull, an instant kill in anything paper-thin like an Astero or Cheetah), the timed rat wave landing on top of you mid-hack, and — same as any wormhole content — whatever else is living in that hole finding you while you're heads-down in a hacking window.

Nullsec via filament — full run

The deep dive. Everything below assumes a 2–4 person crew, no combat fits, one Astero-or-equivalent per person.

Risk: moderate — lower than gating in, since bubble camps and staged gangs never enter the equation, but you're landing in an unknown system with no idea what's already there. Threats: whoever holds the system you land in reacting to local; combat probes appearing on d-scan mid-hack, meaning a hunter has already found your signature; and getting genuinely stuck if the scan for an exit wormhole comes up dry and the hole's own residents start converging.

Noise variants drop you in a low-activity system — fewer defence fleets, fewer explorers who've already cleared it.

The run, in order

01

Entry

Warp somewhere empty — the filament needs 1000km+ from any gate, station or structure. Everyone on grid, then activate. You land together with a 15-minute log-off timer.

02

First sixty seconds

  • Check local. Free intel in null. Twenty names — consider filamenting again.
  • Everyone cloaks, immediately.
  • Everyone makes a safe. Warp between two distant celestials, stop mid-warp, bookmark.
  • Launch probes and scan the system.
03

Scan everything before you hit anything

Tells you what's worth staying for, and where every wormhole is. Relic only — data sites cost more cargo than they pay.

04

Split by system, not by can

Three people on one site splits the same loot three ways. Three people on three systems triples the ground covered. Work nearest cans first; everyone watches local and calls neutrals by name.

05

Moving on

Leave when the relics are done or someone appears in local. Prefer wormholes over gates — nobody camps a hole they don't know exists. Don't roam far; a dry area gets a fresh filament, not five more jumps.

06

Getting home

Filaments are one-way. Assign one person to scan the exit while the others keep working, rotate it. Read every hole's Show Info before jumping — it names the destination class and remaining mass/lifetime. Expect 1–3 jumps. Bookmark every hole you cross. You'll land in a random highsec system — dock, sell locally, don't haul across the map.

If scanning isn't finding an exit

Spare filament

~65-70k ISK, worth carrying one every run. Jump somewhere new rather than spend an hour scanning a bad spot.

Thera / Turnur

Public EVE-Scout hubs. Often the fastest way home if one's nearby.

Pochven filaments — the real mechanics

This is a two-filament trip, not one. Entering and leaving Pochven each need a separate filament, and they're not the same item.

  • 1In: a Pochven entry filament (the "Border" series) — works from any k-space, nullsec included.
  • 2Out, cheap: Glorification 'Devana' extraction filament, seeded in Pochven stations. Dumps you in a random Triglavian-invaded highsec or lowsec system anywhere in the game — no control over where.
  • 3Out, targeted: Proximity Extraction filament, more expensive, lands you in a system adjacent to Pochven of any security status — the version to use if you actually need to land somewhere specific.

Rules that catch people out: you must be in a fleet to activate either filament, even a fleet of one. There's no combat timer — you cannot filament out of an active fight, you need 15 clean minutes first. Same 1000km-from-anything activation rule as the Needlejack. The numbered variants (1/5/10/25) set how many ships travel together, same as Noise-5.

Why it's still the emergency option, not the default: two filaments per person is real cost stacked on top of the Noise-5 you already used to get in, and Glorification lands you somewhere random regardless — you'd still need to scan or fly home from wherever it drops you. Scanning a wormhole chain out is free and usually just as fast. Keep this as the fallback for when scanning genuinely isn't finding an exit, not the plan.

Rules that keep the crew alive

  • 01Cloak whenever idle. Between warps, while deciding, while waiting.
  • 02Warp on first contact. Being wrong costs 30 seconds; being right saves a ship.
  • 03Combat probes on d-scan means you're being hunted. Leave the system.
  • 04Don't all sit on the same grid.
  • 05Nobody rescues anybody. One loss beats three.

Why no dedicated watcher

Nullsec has local chat — free warning wormholes don't give you. A person parked on a gate is wasted throughput here; put everyone on sites and have all of them watch local instead.

Trip shape: ~15min to enter, ~90min–2h working systems, ~30–60min scanning home. Dock, then split loot — don't divide it in space.
